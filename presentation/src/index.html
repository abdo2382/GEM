THE LOST TOMB

An Interactive Journey Through Tutankhamun’s Legacy

Museums Hold Stories. Visitors Need Ways to Discover Them.

  • Tutankhamun’s tomb held more than 5,000 objects, each with its own history.
  • In a gallery, a label has room for a name, a date and a line or two.
  • Visitors who want the story behind an object have to find it somewhere else.

Our solution

Meet THE LOST TOMB

A mobile-first, interactive 3D museum guide.

  • Five cinematic chapters, told as you scroll
  • Treasures you can turn and explore in 3D
  • Harry Burton’s excavation photographs, credited in full
  • English and Arabic, right to left

How it works

The visitor sets the pace

  1. Start journey

    One tap opens the tomb.

  2. Scroll

    The story moves only when the visitor does.

  3. Discover

    Each chapter brings a chamber, a papyrus and an object.

  4. Inspect

    Turn any treasure through 360° and open its details.

  5. Learn

    History, sources and photographs, in English or Arabic.

The cinematic journey

Five chapters, one continuous scroll

  1. The Awakening

    The Valley of the Kings, before 1922

  2. The Discovery

    Sixteen steps to a sealed doorway, 1922

  3. The Treasures

    The Antechamber and the Golden Throne

  4. Inside the Tomb

    The Burial Chamber, opened in 1923

  5. The Living Legacy

    Today, at the Grand Egyptian Museum

The mask moves through every chapter on its own animation rig; the approved 3D model itself is never altered.

Historical authenticity

Real History. Real Evidence.

  • Seven of Harry Burton’s excavation photographs, 1922–23, one on every chapter.
  • Held by Heidelberg University Library; public domain; credited on screen.
  • A manifest records each photo’s subject, creator, holder, source and licence. Automated tests fail if a credit is missing.
  • The story cites its sources. Every 3D object is labelled an artistic reconstruction.

Interactive 3D artifacts

Every treasure, in the visitor’s hands

  • Drag or swipe through 360°
  • Pinch, scroll or tap to zoom
  • Numbered points of interest
  • Burton’s photographs beside the throne
  • Save to the Discovery Trail

All four are artistic reconstructions modelled in code and labelled as such; the mask is fitted to photographs of the original.

Mobile-first

Made for the phone in the visitor’s hand

  • Scroll or swipe between chapters; tap to inspect.
  • The story sheet sits below the mask, never on top of it.
  • Checked from 320 to 1440 px wide; 44 px touch targets.
  • Keyboard and screen-reader support; reduced motion respected.
  • Readable in English and Arabic.
